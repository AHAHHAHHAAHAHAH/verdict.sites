## How the ratio works

A brew ratio compares the weight of ground coffee with the weight of water, or, for espresso, with the weight of the drink in the cup. **1:16** means 1 gram of coffee for every 16 grams of water. One millilitre of water weighs about one gram, so you can read grams of water as millilitres.

## How to use the calculator

1. Pick a method to load a starting ratio, or type your own.
2. Enter the coffee you have, or the amount of drink you want: the other field updates automatically.
3. Brew, taste and adjust. A lower number (for example 1:15) gives a stronger cup, a higher one (1:17) a lighter cup.

## Starting points used here

| Method | Ratio | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Filter / pour-over | 1:18 | About 55 g of coffee per litre, the SCA "Golden Cup" reference |
| French press | 1:15 | Common starting point |
| Espresso | 1:2 | Dose : liquid in the cup |
| Cold brew concentrate | 1:8 | Dilute before drinking |

These are starting points, not rules: beans, grind size and personal taste all move the ideal number.

## Tips for consistent results

- **Weigh instead of using scoops.** The weight of a scoop changes with bean size, roast and grind.
- **For espresso, weigh the drink in the cup**, not the water in the tank.
- **Change one thing at a time**, either the ratio or the grind, so you know what made the difference.

## FAQ

**Is 1:16 better than 1:18?**
Neither is better: 1:16 is stronger, 1:18 is lighter. Start in between and adjust to your taste.

**Why is the espresso ratio so different?**
Because it compares the coffee with the drink that ends up in the cup, not with the water that flows through the machine.
